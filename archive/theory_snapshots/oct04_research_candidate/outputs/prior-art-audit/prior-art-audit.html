DM: ẋᵢ = axᵢ − kxᵢ² + cxᵢ₋₁/(1+xᵢ) − gxᵢ/(1+xᵢ₊q) − hxᵢxᵢ₋q.

PART I — Executive novelty verdict

The project remains a plausible mathematical paper, but its defensible contribution is a constrained classification of modes and interaction-law contrasts—not Fourier analysis, the golden ratio, or the generic existence of rotating waves.

The strongest current package is P05 + P11 + P08. P05 identifies every finite-ring mode that can maximize the real spectrum. P11 then cuts down that list using positivity and the equilibrium relation: some realizable dominant cells cannot contain a physical spectral boundary at all. P08 asks how these possibilities change for a specified bilinear comparator at the same nominal coefficient tuple. The resulting parity and branch restrictions are substantially more informative than a numerical demonstration that two nonlinearities behave differently.

The exact P11 iff criterion and the P08 all-size, branch-labelled comparison theorems are the leading apparent novelty candidates. No materially equivalent theorem was found in the primary sources inspected. This is a MEDIUM-confidence, UNRESOLVED-priority-risk judgment, because the closest five-element proceedings and thesis are not fully accessible. It is not a certificate of first publication. The positive-parameter lift of P05’s cells and switching surfaces is a meaningful supporting result. The bare nearest-cosine calculation is much less distinctive.

A close precedent is the Lorenz-96 equation ẋᵢ=xᵢ₋₁(xᵢ₊₁−xᵢ₋₂)−xᵢ+F. Its homogeneous-state real spectrum is −1+F(cosθ−cos2θ). For F>0, writing u=cosθ yields a concave quadratic with vertex 1/4. The published analysis includes finite-size first-Hopf selection, simultaneous critical pairs and explicit nonlinear criticality. This directly precludes selling a quadratic cosine dispersion or a finite-ring Hopf calculation as a new general method. The relevant locations are Lemma 1, Theorems 2/4 and Proposition 5 in the inspected preprint. [B08: Dirk L. van Kekem, 2018]

Our additional task is different: the DM vertex moves with positive physical parameters, while its diagonal term and equilibrium are constrained by the same parameters. A freely movable quadratic vertex does not establish that all cells lift to positive coefficients, nor that a selected cell can reach r=0. Those are the mathematical distinctions that should carry the paper. P11 expresses the latter obstruction explicitly through wⱼ>0 and max(0,βⱼ)<wⱼ/[4(1−uⱼ)]. The exclusions (8,1) and (12,2) are useful because they separate two notions of realizability within the same proven selection atlas.

Even the conceptual distinction between an algebraic Hopf point and a physically feasible one has prior art. Pais, Caicedo-Núñez and Leonard explicitly discuss infeasible Hopf parameter values for circulant replicator-mutator systems after Theorem 4.5; their Lemma 4.6 also evaluates a Fourier-structured Lyapunov coefficient. This prevents a “first distinction between mathematical and admissible Hopf modes” claim. It does not supply the DM cellwise necessity-and-sufficiency theorem. [B23: Darren Pais, 2012]

P08 has the strongest independent narrative. Its universal forward witness has x*=y*=1, so even equilibrium amplitude is matched at that point. More decisively, the reverse contrast on the specified unique-root domain exists exactly when n≡3 mod 4, while allowing the lower DM branch changes the obstruction to divisibility by four. The independent realization of every ordered pair of reachable dominant representatives with both equilibria stable further separates mode ordering from instability. These exact quantifiers and domain distinctions are what make P08 more than an example.

However, “same topology” must mean the same unsigned directed dependency support in this comparison. At positive states, the DM derivative with respect to xᵢ₊q is positive, while its MB analogue is negative. At q=2 these are distinct edges; for coincident offsets elsewhere, total derivatives must be combined. The comparison changes effective regulatory signs as well as receiver dependence and boundary behavior. Consequently the conclusions establish a property of these two specified laws, not an isolated biological effect of saturation with an unchanged signed interaction graph. Fixing nominal coefficients also presupposes the declared dimensionless state and time conventions.

P10 is the strongest supporting steady-state theorem. The useful calculation is Gf−Hf=rf(k+c/sf²)>0 at every positive fold, for every allowed n,q. That identity forces all transverse real parts strictly negative without extra parameter hypotheses. The subsequent simple-zero fold theorem, local sink–saddle pair, persistence of sinks and local separatrix use established machinery. Broader cyclic-feedback literature already studies bistability and basin organization; its single-input monotonicity assumptions do not automatically hold for DM. [B25: Jules Guilberteau, 2023] [B34: Yuri A. Kuznetsov, 2006]

The remaining current modules should mostly support those headline results. P01 establishes a sound phase space and bounds. Its exact-radius finite-entry iff statement is a small model-specific refinement; the maximum comparison method is routine, and the blueprint expressly does not claim an optimal radius. P02 is exact root algebra on synchrony. P03–P04 are differentiation and standard circulant spectral theory. P07 is a useful five-node worked example, especially for stable ties, but fifth-root golden-ratio identities are not a new biological principle. An earlier five-agent Wu-Xing paper explicitly emphasizes golden-ratio structure. [B06: Ciann-Dong Yang, 2023] [B17: Robert M. Gray, 2006]

P09’s full-system transcritical statement is stronger than a scalar sketch, but its ingredients are an invariant center line, a simple uniform zero and a transverse implicit-function argument. That makes it a correct routine application rather than a new bifurcation mechanism. The degenerate cubic locus should stay separately stated. P10 does not justify stability of the upper branch, index one of the lower branch, or exclusivity of two attractors throughout the entire two-root interval; the blueprint already contains an exact counterexample.

Future P12–P14 have unequal value. A nonzero total crossing derivative on the frozen g-path is necessary infrastructure. A nonzero first Lyapunov coefficient converts the spectral candidate into a classified nonlinear local bifurcation and can materially improve completeness, even if the calculation itself adds little methodological novelty. The best P13 target is an explicit sign theorem on a substantial admissible part of the Hopf surface, indexed by size or mode and connected to the P11 restrictions. The existence of some small open neighborhood of one nonzero value follows from continuity; that alone is not a strong region theorem. Fourier contraction through modes 0 and 2m already appears in generalized Lorenz-96 work. [B11: John Kerin, 2022] [B21: Karen M. Page, 2018]

Once a simple nondegenerate Hopf branch is proved, P14’s exact cyclic phase relation, cluster count and signed propagation are essentially consequences of equivariant Hopf theory. Stewart’s recent ring treatment gives both general phase-pattern statements and a direction criterion. The contribution of this project would be identifying which physically feasible DM modes actually produce those waves and on which parameter side, rather than introducing a new rotating-wave theorem. [B12: Ian Stewart, 2024] [B16: Martin Golubitsky, 1985]

The biggest priority issue remains the Lin–Shi–Han line. A 2020 bilinear five-element equation was inspected; it is not DM. The accessible later manuscript excerpt uses enzyme activation/inactivation fractions, again visibly different in those terms. But the 2021–2023 proceedings, the complete 2024 main text and Lin’s 2021 master’s thesis have not all been checked. The retrieved 2024 supplement refers to a Hopf parameter region, so “those papers are only numerical” is not defensible. The proper overall exact-model verdict is PRIORITY STATUS UNRESOLVED. [B01: Haipeng Lin, 2020] [B02: Haipeng Lin, 2021] [B03: Haipeng Lin, 2022] [B04: Haipeng Lin, 2023] [B05: Haipeng Lin, 2024]

My recommended paper story is: “Exact finite-ring mode selection and physical criticality constraints under two specified interaction laws.” It can connect dominance, admissible criticality, same-tuple contrasts and the steady-state branch structure in a coherent theorem sequence. Publication-worthiness is conditional on the underlying proofs and the remaining priority checks; it should not be inferred from the sheer number of modules. A compact paper led by P08 and P11, with P05 as their common geometric foundation and P10 as branch organization, is more persuasive than fourteen equally weighted novelty claims.

PART IV — Deep audits of P05, P08, P10 and P11

P05: how much of the selection atlas survives an adversarial comparison?

KNOWN RESULT: finite-ring spectra can be optimized on roots of unity, with a particularly close quadratic-cosine example in Lorenz-96. OUR RESULT: a moving positive vertex and a complete physical parameter atlas. MINIMAL DIFFERENCE: the variable vertex, sparse law and physical lift. The elementary optimization is a small difference; the parameter realization and its relation to criticality are structurally meaningful.

The reduction itself explains why the nearest cosine rule is routine. For D=H−G>0,

r(u)=d+D+C²/(8D)−2D(u−z)²,   z=C/(4D)>0.
r(uⱼ)−r(uⱼ₊₁)=C(uⱼ−uⱼ₊₁)(1−χβⱼ),   χ=4D/C.

This is one-dimensional nearest-point geometry. The midpoint equation establishes adjacency of maximal ties; three distinct cosines cannot all be equidistant from one real vertex. Once z is restricted to the positive half-line, the endpoint count J and its residues modulo four follow from the cosine grid. Calling this a new spectral optimization technique would be too strong. Calling it a complete exact classification for this constrained model is defensible.

The stronger statement is constructive: prescribed z,x,c,g determine h, while k can be chosen to ensure positive a through the equilibrium relation. The regular physical chart then transfers strict inequalities to open parameter regions and ties to smooth hypersurfaces. This solves a feasibility problem left out of an unconstrained dispersion plot. Stewart’s arbitrary-order theorem does not imply it: allowing every connection coefficient independently is a different parameter class. [B12: Ian Stewart, 2024]

Stable switching deserves a short proposition, not a headline. Adding sufficiently strong negative diagonal contribution can make an entire finite family of modes stable while their ordering changes. In DM that diagonal change must be accompanied by the correct equilibrium-preserving adjustment of a. The construction is useful evidence that the selection atlas is not itself a bifurcation diagram.

MINIMAL DIFFERENCE: a necessary-and-sufficient inequality depending only on the discrete mode geometry, with constructive lifting. That is a meaningful theorem-level difference, although exact-model priority is unresolved.

kx = D wⱼ − C[1−uⱼ+x/(1+x)],

This inequality exposes what an arbitrary diagonal shift would hide: the physical k cannot be negative. Letting x range over positive values and intersecting the admissible z-interval with the strict Voronoi cell produces

wⱼ>0 and Lⱼ<Bⱼ,   Lⱼ=max(0,βⱼ),   Bⱼ=wⱼ/[4(1−uⱼ)].

Sufficiency also needs positive a and nonzero frequency. The blueprint supplies those by choosing G large enough and excluding at most one zero-frequency value. Thus this is more than a necessary inequality derived from a dispersion relation. The pair is individually simple because all other representatives have strictly smaller real parts and its two values are distinct when ωⱼ≠0. That last implication is routine; the positive lift is the substantive analysis.

The two explicit failures are different. For (8,1), wⱼ=0 precludes positive k at zero abscissa. For (12,2), wⱼ>0 but the lower strict cell endpoint equals Bⱼ=1/4, so the required open intersection is empty. A table that records only wⱼ>0 would miss the second obstruction. These cells can still dominate, including at stable equilibria. That concrete separation is more persuasive than claiming that positivity restrictions themselves are new.

Angeli–Banaji–Pantea use additive-compound and signed-graph structure to exclude Hopf in broader interaction classes. Conversely, merely citing their framework would not prove that DM’s iff is already known more generally. [B24: David Angeli, 2014]

The reverse theorem must be split. On C₊={M>0,μ>0}, with M=k+g+h−c, both positive branches are uniquely named, and reverse stability occurs iff n≡3 mod 4. The independent stable-mode realization is particularly valuable. It fixes the same tuple while permitting any ordered pair of reachable strict representatives, even when both equilibria are stable. That says the two selection maps are not locked together by the matching constraint. It is logically different from, and stronger in a different direction than, one stability reversal.

PART V — Future literature risk: P12–P14

P12: necessary exact verification, limited independent novelty

The intended path fixes a,k,c,h and varies g. The equilibrium moves. The correct calculation starts from E(x,g)=a−(k+h)x+(c−g)/(1+x)=0 and differentiates E along the chosen smooth branch. Then differentiate rⱼ(x(g),g). An equilibrium-coordinate derivative such as ∂ₖRⱼ=−x is a different direction because maintaining that chart also changes a. A generic perturbation theorem is likewise insufficient for the prescribed physical path. [B13: Ian Stewart, 2024] [B33: Yuri A. Kuznetsov, 2006]

Recommended target: exact crossing sign at the algebraic candidate; a rigorously specified nearby interval retaining positivity, branch identity, strict mode selection and stable complement; an explicit statement of the unstable side. This is projected ROUTINE APPLICATION, HIGH confidence / LOW methodological priority risk. A theorem for a whole admissible family of crossings would be more useful but should not delay the essential pointwise calculation. Neither target is presently proved.

P13: pursue interpretation of the sign, not expression length

The likely computation is efficient because translation symmetry imposes mode addition modulo n. The quadratic terms feeding the Hopf coefficient pass through modes 0 and 2m, and the cubic term returns to m. Existing ring papers already exploit this. DM’s rational terms contribute genuine higher derivatives; they prevent simply copying a quadratic-model coefficient. Normalize the Fourier vectors, Taylor factorials, adjoint inner product and positive-frequency choice before comparing formulas. [B11: John Kerin, 2022] [B33: Yuri A. Kuznetsov, 2006]

Comparable work has gone beyond a single sign check: biological repression models obtain criticality conditions involving derivatives of the feedback function, and circulant evolutionary models express criticality across parameter families. These are appropriate standards for an ambitious P13 result. [B21: Karen M. Page, 2018] [B23: Darren Pais, 2012]

  1. Minimum useful completion: an exact nonzero l₁ at P11’s five-node candidate, plus P12, yields a rigorous local nonlinear existence/classification theorem. This materially improves completeness, but contributes limited standalone novelty.
  2. Preferred target: a tractable formula on the admissible Hopf surface and a proved sign on an explicitly described substantial region, with n/mode restrictions. A modest formula with a strong inequality is better than a very long algebraic expression without interpretation.
  3. Stronger connection: relate criticality to the P11 cell constraints—e.g. determine whether all candidates in a named mode family share one sign, or exhibit rigorously separated sign regimes within the admissible domain.
  4. Optional law comparison: compare DM and MB on explicitly defined Hopf surfaces or crossing paths. Same nominal coefficients need not put both models at Hopf simultaneously; do not compare an l₁ at Hopf with an off-boundary quantity.

An unspecified open neighborhood with fixed sign follows automatically from a nonzero value and continuity. A meaningful region theorem needs explicit domain, parameter margins or size/mode coverage. An exact closed form is useful for reproducibility but is not a substitute for such a theorem. A zero of l₁ supplies only a possible generalized-Hopf location; proving a Bautin bifurcation requires the additional higher-order and unfolding conditions.

P14: exact symmetry is standard, its physically realized instance is useful

After the simple nondegenerate branch exists, cyclic equivariance determines its spatiotemporal symmetry. With the blueprint’s signed eigenvalue convention, the anticipated adjacent-node relation is xᵢ₊₁(t)=xᵢ(t+sgn(ωₘ)mT(ε)/n), modulo the nonlinear period and subject to a consistent choice of Fourier representative. There are n/gcd(m,n) distinct phases, with gcd(m,n) nodes in each phase class. The leading cosine and the exact shift identity are different assertions. [B12: Ian Stewart, 2024] [B16: Martin Golubitsky, 1985]

Recommended target: a short corollary stating the actual nonlinear period, signed shift, spatial kernel and orbital stability inherited from the Hopf theorem. Do not say that the graph’s arrow orientation alone determines wave direction. Do not claim constant period 2π/ωH away from the bifurcation. Generic rotating waves add little novelty; the novel content can be which positive-parameter mode cells support a proved stable or unstable DM wave.

PART XI — Recommended manuscript positioning

Recommended title: Finite-ring mode selection and physical criticality constraints in demand-modulated cyclic networks.

Contribution paragraph, using only current results: We study homogeneous equilibria of a directed cyclic network with receiver-dependent input and compare them with a specified bilinear network sharing the same directed dependency support and nominal coefficients. For step-two coupling, we classify the complete finite-size dominant-mode cells and prove their realization by positive parameters. We then derive a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for a strict nonuniform cell to contain a physical spectral critical point, identifying selectable modes that cannot become critical. Matched-law comparisons yield exact stability reversals, residue-class and branch restrictions, and independent realization of dominant modes at stable equilibria. Full-system transcritical and saddle-node analysis organizes the synchronous branches, including automatic transverse stability at every positive fold. These results distinguish spectral ordering, admissible criticality and interaction-law dependence; nonlinear Hopf and rotating-wave conclusions require the additional calculations specified separately.

Six principal contributions:

  1. The complete finite-n q=2 selection atlas with physical cell and switch realization.
  2. The exact P11 positivity-constrained candidate criterion and explicit obstruction cells.
  3. The all-n same-tuple forward contrast and the exact reverse parity/branch distinctions.
  4. Independent realization of every reachable ordered pair of dominant representatives with both models stable.
  5. The universal transverse-fold identity and a certified open region of coexistence.
  6. The exact five-node spectral candidate, to be upgraded to a nonlinear Hopf/wave theorem only after P12–P14.

Main text: model/comparator definition, concise framework, P05–P11 geometric relationship, P08 comparisons, branch-labelled P09/P10 organization, counterexamples and one five-node illustration. Give the closest-prior differences explicitly in the introduction.

Appendices: extended P01 edge cases, complete negative-root tables, Jacobian collision bookkeeping, full P07 ordering/coincidence table, long constructive algebra, future tensor derivatives and normalization checks. P06 belongs here unless it reveals a genuinely additional phenomenon.

Not novelty: a Fourier eigenbasis, invariant synchrony, generic local bifurcation theorems, the textbook l₁ formula or the equivariant Hopf conclusion. Use citations and short supporting statements.
