\section{Layer A: fold geometry and complete spectrum}
\label{sec:A}
\subsection{Original restriction, notation and exact roots (checks 3--5)}
From the canonical field
\begin{equation}
 F_i(x)=a x_i-kx_i^2+\frac{c x_{i-1}}{1+x_i}
       -\frac{g x_i}{1+x_{i+q}}-h x_i x_{i-q},
 \label{eq:original}
\end{equation}
substitute $x=r\one$, $r>-1$. All five terms must be retained even when
neighbor offsets coincide. Each component equals
\[
 f(r,g)=ar-(k+h)r^2+\frac{(c-g)r}{1+r}
       =\frac r{1+r}\{\mu+br-Ar^2\},
 \quad A=k+h,\quad b=a-A,\quad\mu=a+c-g.
\]
Thus synchrony is invariant by uniqueness. With $b>0$,
\[
 g_f=a+c+\frac{b^2}{4A}
     =c+\frac{(a+A)^2}{4A},\qquad
 r_f=\frac b{2A},\qquad s_f=1+r_f=\frac{a+A}{2A}.
\]
The second expression for $g_f$ follows from
$4Aa+(a-A)^2=(a+A)^2$; also $g_f-g_c=b^2/(4A)>0$.
P02's amplitudes $s_\pm$ are P10's $r_\pm$. P02's fold amplitude
$s_f$ is P10's $r_f$, whereas P10's $s_f$ is the denominator.
The source reconciles these explicitly in lines 31--36; no formula imports
the wrong meaning.

Let $u=r-r_f$ and $\nu=g_f-g$. Since
$\mu=\nu-b^2/(4A)$, $br_f-Ar_f^2=b^2/(4A)$ and $b-2Ar_f=0$,
\begin{align}
 \mu+b(r_f+u)-A(r_f+u)^2&=\nu-Au^2,\\
 \dot u&=\frac{r_f+u}{s_f+u}(\nu-Au^2).
 \label{eq:exact}
\end{align}
These are identities, not approximations. On $|u|<r_f/2$, neither prefactor
vanishes. For $\nu<0$ the polynomial is negative for every real $u$, so there
is no nonzero synchronous equilibrium, including near the fold. At $\nu=0$
there is one repeated positive root. For $\nu>0$ the two distinct roots are
$r_\pm=r_f\pm\sqrt{\nu/A}$; they are both positive exactly when
$0<\nu<A r_f^2=b^2/(4A)$. A sufficiently small positive $\nu$ places both
inside the chosen state neighborhood. The ever-present origin is excluded
by that neighborhood; it is not removed from the full system.

\subsection{Physical fold identity and phase signs (checks 6--7)}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:original} coordinate by coordinate at synchrony gives
\[
 d=a-2kr-\frac{cr}{(1+r)^2}-\frac g{1+r}-hr,\quad
 C=\frac c{1+r},\quad G=\frac{gr}{(1+r)^2},\quad H=hr.
\]
For $(Pz)_j=z_{j-1}$, the $i-1$ input is $CP$, the $i+q$ loss derivative is
$+GP^{-q}$, and the $i-q$ bilinear derivative is $-HP^q$. Thus
\[
 J=dI+CP+GP^{-q}-HP^q,
 \qquad \lambda_m=d+Ce^{-\ii\theta_m}
                 +Ge^{\ii q\theta_m}-He^{-\ii q\theta_m},
 \quad\theta_m=2\pi m/n.
\]
The sign on $G$ is positive because differentiating the reciprocal loss
denominator reverses its sign. The imaginary $H$ contribution is positive
because $-H$ multiplies a negative sine. Offset collisions add coefficients.

The decisive fold identity follows directly from physical values:
\[
 g_f=c+A s_f^2,\qquad
 \frac{g_f}{s_f^2}-h=A-h+\frac c{s_f^2}=k+\frac c{s_f^2}>0.
\]
Multiplying by $r_f>0$ proves
\[
 \boxed{G_f-H_f=r_f\left(k+\frac c{s_f^2}\right)
 =r_f\left(k+\frac{4A^2c}{(a+A)^2}\right)>0.}
\]
At a nonzero equilibrium $f_r=r(b-2Ar)/(1+r)$; hence
$\lambda_0^f=f_r(r_f,g_f)=0$ and $d_f=-C_f-G_f+H_f$. Subtracting this row
sum gives
\begin{align*}
 \Ree\lambda_m^f&=C_f(\cos\theta_m-1)
             +(G_f-H_f)(\cos(q\theta_m)-1),\\
 \Imm\lambda_m^f&=-C_f\sin\theta_m+(G_f+H_f)\sin(q\theta_m).
\end{align*}

\subsection{Automatic gap and simple zero (checks 8--9)}
For $1\le m<n$, $j=\min(m,n-m)$ satisfies
$2\pi j/n\in[2\pi/n,\pi]$. Cosine is decreasing there, so
\[
 \cos\theta_m\le\cos(2\pi/n)<1,\qquad \cos(q\theta_m)\le1.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \Ree\lambda_m^f\le-\delta_f<0,\qquad
 \delta_f=\frac c{s_f}\{1-\cos(2\pi/n)\}.
\]
For odd $n$ the last angle is below $\pi$; for even $n$ it may equal $\pi$.
Both cases obey the same inequality. At $q=1$ or $q=n-1$, combined shifts
still have the displayed spectrum. At even $n$, $q=n/2$, the two $q$ shifts
coincide but their difference is retained. If $\gcd(n,q)>1$, some nonuniform
labels can have $\cos(q\theta_m)=1$; the strictly negative first term still
provides the entire margin. No coprimality assumption is needed.

The unitary Fourier matrix has $n$ independent columns and diagonalizes $J_f$.
Exactly one diagonal entry is zero and all others have negative real part.
Thus zero is the only imaginary-axis eigenvalue, has algebraic and geometric
multiplicity one, and has real eigenspace $\operatorname{span}\{\one\}$.
Coincidences among stable numerical eigenvalues do not change this conclusion.
The gap is positive for each fixed $n$; it need not remain bounded below as
$n\to\infty$. \textbf{FOLD GEOMETRY / SPECTRUM VERDICT: VERIFIED.}

\section{Layer B: full-system saddle-node}
\label{sec:B}
\subsection{Real transverse IFT and equilibrium completeness (checks 10--11)}
Take $v=\one$, $w^{\mathsf T}=n^{-1}\one^{\mathsf T}$,
$E=\ker w^{\mathsf T}$ and $\Pi_\perp=I-vw^{\mathsf T}$. Then
$x=r\one+y$ is a unique decomposition with $y\in E$. Every circulant row
sum and column sum is $d+C+G-H$, so $J_f E\subset E$. The complexification
of $E$ is spanned by the nonuniform Fourier vectors. Its spectrum is strictly
left, so its real restriction $B_f=J_f|_E$ is invertible: any real kernel
vector would also lie in the zero complex kernel, and injectivity in finite
dimension implies surjectivity.

For $T(r,y,g)=\Pi_\perp F(r\one+y,g)$,
$D_yT(r_f,0,g_f)=\Pi_\perp J_f|_E=B_f$. The real IFT gives open
neighborhoods $I_r,I_g,U_E$ and one smooth $\psi:I_r\times I_g\to U_E$,
with $T=0$ in their product if and only if $y=\psi(r,g)$. Choose $U_E$
containing zero and shrink the parameter product if necessary. Synchrony
supplies $T(r,0,g)=0$ for every pair in that same product. Uniqueness therefore
forces $\psi(r,g)=0$ for every pair, not just at the fold.

Every nearby full equilibrium solves $T=0$, hence has $y=0$. Its remaining
mean equation is $f(r,g)=0$; for the selected $r>0$, this is exactly
$\nu-Au^2=0$. Conversely its roots do solve all components. A state ball
around $r_f\one$ times a small $g$ interval fits inside the product. This
proves local completeness and excludes hidden nonsynchronous equilibria.

\subsection{Nullvectors and original-field derivatives (checks 12--14)}
Each shift has both row and column sum one. Therefore
\[
 J_fv=0,\qquad w^{\mathsf T}J_f=0,\qquad w^{\mathsf T}v=1.
\]
Holding $a,k,c,h$ fixed in \eqref{eq:original},
$(F_g)_i=-x_i/(1+x_{i+q})$, hence
\[
 \alpha_{\rm SN}=w^{\mathsf T}F_g=-r_f/s_f<0.
\]
For $g=g_f-\nu$, $F_\nu=-F_g$ and the coefficient is $+r_f/s_f$.

Along $x=(r_f+t)\one$ the original terms have second derivatives
\[
 0,\quad-2k,\quad-2c/s_f^3,\quad+2g_f/s_f^3,\quad-2h.
\]
In particular $d^2(r/(1+r))/dr^2=-2/(1+r)^3$ establishes both rational
terms independently. Their sum is
\[
 -2(k+h)+\frac{2(g_f-c)}{s_f^3}
 =-2A+\frac{2A}{s_f}=-\frac{2Ar_f}{s_f}.
\]
It is the same in each of the $n$ components. The mean $w^{\mathsf T}$
therefore introduces no additional $n$ factor. The full contraction and
half-Taylor coefficient are respectively
\[
 w^{\mathsf T}F_{xx}[v,v]=-\frac{2Ar_f}{s_f},\qquad
 \beta_{\rm SN}=\frac12w^{\mathsf T}F_{xx}[v,v]
              =-\frac{Ar_f}{s_f}<0.
\]
These computations retain all original terms at every offset collision.

\subsection{Extended center dynamics and time orientation (checks 15--18)}
In $(u,y,\nu)$ coordinates for $(\dot x,\dot\nu)=(F(x,g_f-\nu),0)$,
$F_\nu=(r_f/s_f)\one$ at the fold, its transverse projection vanishes,
and $w^{\mathsf T}J_f=0$. The linearization is consequently
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&0&r_f/s_f\\
 0&B_f&0\\
 0&0&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
On the $(u,\nu)$ plane it is a nonzero nilpotent block with a double
algebraic zero and one-dimensional kernel. This is the \emph{extended}
zero multiplicity; the fixed-parameter Jacobian still has a simple zero.
The generalized center space is exactly $y=0$. Unlike P09 at the origin,
the parameter derivative here does not vanish.

The set $y=0$ with variable $(u,\nu)$ is smooth and invariant by synchrony
and $\dot\nu=0$, and is tangent to the whole generalized center space.
It is thus an exact local two-dimensional center manifold. At fixed $\nu=0$
its state slice is the exact one-dimensional center manifold. This uses
invariance and tangency, not equilibrium reduction alone. Its restriction
is \eqref{eq:exact} together with $\dot\nu=0$.

For $|u|<r_f/2$,
\[
 \frac{r_f+u}{s_f+u}\ge\frac{r_f/2}{1+3r_f/2}>0.
\]
Thus $d\tau/dt=(r_f+u)/(s_f+u)$ is smooth and orientation preserving,
and division yields exactly
\[
 \frac{du}{d\tau}=\nu-Au^2,\qquad\frac{d\nu}{d\tau}=0.
\]
Simple fixed-parameter zero, stable complement, nonzero $\alpha_{\rm SN}$
and $\beta_{\rm SN}$, local equilibrium completeness, and this exact center
dynamics jointly justify the nondegenerate full-system saddle-node label.
The side $g>g_f$ has no equilibria in the fold neighborhood; $g=g_f$ has
a repeated reduced root; $g<g_f$ sufficiently close has precisely two positive
equilibria. ``Double equilibrium'' here does not assert a double eigenvalue
of the fixed-parameter Jacobian. The normal form does not decouple the entire
nonlinear system globally.

\subsection{The fold itself is not a sink (check 19)}
At $\nu=0$ write $q(u)=(r_f+u)/(s_f+u)>0$. Then
$\dot u=-Aq(u)u^2$. Small $u>0$ decreases to zero, by monotonicity and
uniqueness; small $u<0$ decreases away from zero. On a fixed compact negative
interval, $q\ge q_*>0$, so $v=-u$ satisfies $\dot v\ge Aq_*v^2$ until
exit. Arbitrarily small negative initial perturbations exit a prescribed
small neighborhood in finite time. They lie within the positive orthant for
a sufficiently small neighborhood. Thus the fold is not Lyapunov stable,
and in particular is not locally asymptotically stable in the open state
space. \textbf{FULL-SYSTEM SADDLE-NODE VERDICT: VERIFIED.}

\section{Layer C: branch stability and open coexistence}
\label{sec:C}
\subsection{Whole-interval uniform signs (checks 20--21)}
For $g_c<g<g_f$,
\[
 \Delta^2=b^2+4A(a+c-g)=4A(g_f-g),\qquad 0<\Delta<b,
 \qquad r_\pm=\frac{b\pm\Delta}{2A}>0.
\]
Therefore $b-2Ar_\pm=\mp\Delta$. At the roots the polynomial factor
in $f$ vanishes, so differentiation leaves only
$f_r=r(b-2Ar)/(1+r)$. Hence
\[
 \lambda_0(r_-)=\frac{r_-\Delta}{1+r_-}>0,\qquad
 \lambda_0(r_+)=-\frac{r_+\Delta}{1+r_+}<0.
\]
These signs are valid on the entire open interval, not merely asymptotically
near the fold. The lower equilibrium is nonlinearly unstable even if another
mode happens to be nonhyperbolic; a strictly positive eigenvalue suffices.
A loss of upper stability within this interval cannot be a uniform-mode loss.

\subsection{Finite-label persistence and dimensions (checks 22--24)}
Every labeled $\lambda_m(r,g)$ is smooth near $(r_f,g_f)$, and there are
only finitely many labels for fixed $n$. Intersect their neighborhoods so
$|\Ree\lambda_m(r,g)-\Ree\lambda_m^f|<\delta_f/2$ for $m\ne0$.
Both $r_\pm(\nu)$ are continuous at $\nu=0$; differentiability in $\nu$
is not required. Some $\varepsilon_{\rm sp}>0$ therefore puts both branches
in this common neighborhood for $0\le\nu<\varepsilon_{\rm sp}$, with
\[
 \Ree\lambda_m(r_\pm,g_f-\nu)<-\delta_f/2\quad(m\ne0).
\]
Shrink the interval to preserve root positivity. For $\nu>0$ there is exactly
one positive eigenvalue on the lower branch, the uniform one. It is simple
because every other label is strictly stable. There are $n-1$ stable real
dimensions and one unstable real dimension. Each nonreal conjugate pair
contributes two real dimensions, as do two stable labels with coinciding
real eigenvalue; multiplicity is not discarded. The upper branch is Hurwitz,
hence locally exponentially stable. No maximizing-mode theorem is involved.

\subsection{Origin, parameter openness and meaning of bistability (checks 25--28)}
Directly at the origin $J(0)=(a-g)I+cP$, so
\[
 \Ree\lambda_m(0)=a+c-g-c(1-\cos\theta_m)
 \le g_c-g<0.
\]
This is independent of $q$ and proves exponential stability throughout the
whole two-positive-root interval, in the local open extension and relative
to the invariant nonnegative orthant. Choose any
$0<\varepsilon<\min\{\varepsilon_{\rm sp},b^2/(4A)\}$.
The interval $(g_f-\varepsilon,g_f)$ is nonempty, lies above $g_c$, and has
the origin sink, lower index-one saddle and upper sink simultaneously.

At a strict witness each Jacobian is invertible and hyperbolic, and the three
equilibria are distinct. The IFT continues them smoothly under changes in
$(a,k,c,g,h)$. The origin remains exactly zero. The two positive roots are
also explicit smooth functions because their discriminant is strictly
positive, so local uniqueness identifies these with the IFT continuations.
After shrinking a parameter neighborhood, root separation, root positivity,
coefficient positivity, $a>k+h$, $g_c<g<g_f$ and all strict spectral signs
persist. This is an open set in the five-dimensional physical parameter
space for each fixed $(n,q)$, not merely an open segment in $g$.
Dependence of its size on $(n,q)$ is expressly allowed.

P10 defines bistability as coexistence of at least two locally stable
equilibria. Its statements are consistent with that definition: they neither
count all attractors nor exclude periodic or other invariant attracting sets.

\section{Layer D: exact coexistence set and counterexample}
\label{sec:D}
\subsection{Openness and the two directions of exactness (checks 29--30)}
Let
\[
 \mathcal D=\{(a,k,c,g,h)\in(0,\infty)^5:
                  a>k+h,\quad g_c<g<g_f\}.
\]
It is open since the functions and inequalities are continuous and strict.
On it $r_+=(b+\sqrt{b^2+4A(a+c-g)})/(2A)$ is smooth: $A>0$, the
radicand is positive, and $1+r_+>0$. Thus each labeled eigenvalue is smooth.
The maximum of finitely many continuous real parts is continuous even at
mode ties, so
\[
 \mathcal B_{n,q}=\{\pi\in\mathcal D:
                 \max_m\Ree\lambda_m(r_+,g)<0\}
\]
is open; the near-fold witnesses make it nonempty.

Every member has two local exponential sinks since the origin is Hurwitz
throughout $\mathcal D$ and so is the upper root on $\mathcal B_{n,q}$.
For the converse, use the standard meaning of local exponential stability:
there exist $K,\eta,\delta>0$ such that
\[
 \norm{\Phi_t(p+v)-p}\le K e^{-\eta t}\norm v
 \quad(t\ge0,\ \norm v<\delta).
\]
For each fixed $t$, smooth flow dependence gives
$D\Phi_t(p)=e^{tJ}$. Substitute $v=\epsilon z$, divide by $|\epsilon|$,
and let $\epsilon\to0$ to obtain
$\norm{e^{tJ}z}\le K e^{-\eta t}\norm z$ with the same constants for all
$t\ge0$. The induced semigroup bound implies
$\max\Ree\operatorname{spec}(J)\le-\eta<0$, for example by spectral
mapping and the spectral-radius bound on the matrix norm. The upper root is
interior to the orthant, so a relative exponential estimate also applies to
an ordinary full ball there. This proves both directions of ``exactly.''
Nonhyperbolic asymptotically stable boundary points are not classified.

\subsection{The sufficient inequality and its explicit interval (checks 31--32)}
At any synchronous state subtract the spectral row sum:
\[
 \Ree\lambda_m=\lambda_0+C(\cos\theta_m-1)
                         +(G-H)(\cos(q\theta_m)-1).
\]
Here $\lambda_0$ means the uniform row sum; its simplified polynomial-root
formula is used only at equilibria. On the upper root $\lambda_0<0$.
If $G\ge H$, the two remaining contributions are nonpositive. For $m\ne0$
the $C$ term is strictly negative, and for $m=0$ the already negative uniform
value suffices. Equality $G=H$ is therefore included. Since $r_+>0$,
$G\ge H$ is equivalent to $g/(1+r_+)^2\ge h$.

Put
\[
 M=g_f-hs_f^2=c+ks_f^2>0,\qquad
 t_* =\min\left\{\frac{r_f}{2},\frac{M}{4hs_f},
                  \sqrt{\frac{M}{2(A+h)}}\right\}>0.
\]
For $0<\nu<At_*^2$ set $t=\sqrt{\nu/A}$, so $0<t<t_*$,
$g=g_f-At^2$ and $1+r_+=s_f+t$. Direct expansion gives
\[
 g-h(1+r_+)^2=M-2hs_ft-(A+h)t^2.
\]
The strict inequality $t<t_*\le M/(4hs_f)$ gives
$2hs_ft<M/2$; likewise $t<t_*\le\sqrt{M/[2(A+h)]}$ gives
$(A+h)t^2<M/2$. Their sum is strictly less than $M$, so the displayed
quantity is positive. Moreover,
\[
 At_*^2\le\frac{A r_f^2}{4}=\frac{b^2}{16A}
             <\frac{b^2}{4A}=g_f-g_c.
\]
Thus $(g_f-At_*^2,g_f)$ is a nonempty sufficient stable-coexistence interval
independent of $(n,q)$. This assertion concerns the two sinks only; to
retain lower index one, intersect it with the previous transverse-persistence
interval. The source makes that distinction explicitly.

\section{Layer E: basin thresholds and the local stable disk}
\label{sec:E}
\subsection{Global threshold within the synchronous ray (checks 37--38)}
For every $g_c<g<g_f$ the exact factorization is
\[
 f(r,g)=-\frac{Ar}{1+r}(r-r_-)(r-r_+).
\]
For positive $r$ the prefactor is negative. Therefore the signs on
$(0,r_-)$, $(r_-,r_+)$ and $(r_+,\infty)$ are respectively negative,
positive and negative. Smooth uniqueness prevents crossing an equilibrium.
P01 supplies global nonnegative solutions and boundedness; here monotonicity
also gives direct bounds. A solution in $(0,r_-)$ decreases and is bounded
below by zero; one between the roots increases and stays below $r_+$; one
above $r_+$ decreases and stays above it. Each has a limit. If $f$ at that
limit were nonzero, continuity would bound its magnitude away from zero
with fixed sign near the limit, contradicting convergence. The initial
strict inequalities exclude the wrong endpoint. Hence
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{initial amplitude}&\text{limit}\\\hline
0&0\text{ (constant)}\\
0<r(0)<r_-&0\\
r(0)=r_-&r_-\text{ (constant)}\\
r_-<r(0)<r_+&r_+\\
r(0)=r_+&r_+\text{ (constant)}\\
r(0)>r_+&r_+
\end{array}
\]
These are exact full-system solutions by invariance. The conclusion holds
even where the upper root is transversely unstable and makes no statement
about nonsynchronous points there.

\subsection{Manifold existence and exact unstable germ (checks 39--41)}
Fix a near-fold tuple for which $p=r_-\one$ is a hyperbolic index-one saddle
and both $0$ and $r_+\one$ are sinks. The smooth stable/unstable manifold
theorem applies in an open neighborhood of $p$ inside the positive orthant.
Its stable tangent space is $E$ with dimension $n-1$ and its unstable space
is $\operatorname{span}\{\one\}$ with dimension one.

To establish equality of the unstable germ with synchrony, write
$s=r-r_-$ on the synchronous line. There
$\dot s=s(\lambda+\eta(s))$, with $\lambda=f_r(r_-,g)>0$ and
$\eta(0)=0$. Choose a small interval $|s|<\rho_0$ contained in
$0<r<r_+$ and with $|\eta|<\lambda/2$. In reverse time $\tau=-t$,
$|s|$ decreases at least exponentially at rate $\lambda/2$; it stays in
the interval for all $\tau\ge0$ and tends to zero. The lifted vector
trajectory has the same property. Thus every point of this synchronous
interval belongs to the local unstable set. The uniqueness and backward-orbit
characterization of the unstable manifold, or the local inclusion of two
one-dimensional embedded manifolds through $p$, makes their germs equal.
Tangency alone is not the argument.

The left branch stays in $0<r<r_-$ and has forward limit zero; the right
branch stays in $r_-<r<r_+$ and has forward limit $r_+$. Their negative-time
limit is $p$. They are therefore synchronous heteroclinic trajectories of
the original full system. Their forward continuation need not remain inside
the chosen local saddle chart.

\subsection{A legitimate chart and exact equations (checks 42--45)}
By the graph form of the stable manifold, in coordinates
$x=p+s\one+y$, $y\in E$, its germ is $s=\phi(y)$, where
$\phi(0)=0$ and $D\phi(0)=0$. Define
\[
 \xi=s-\phi(y),\quad z=y,\qquad
 x=p+(\xi+\phi(z))\one+z.
\]
The triangular transformation has an explicit smooth inverse on its chart.
The stable disk becomes $\xi=0$. Since $z=0$ means $y=0$ and
$\phi(0)=0$, the synchronous unstable segment remains $z=0$.
At the origin the linearized coordinate transformation is the identity in
$(s,y)$, so the linear blocks remain $\lambda>0$ and $B=J(p)|_E$.

Let $X(\xi,z)$ denote the transformed $\dot\xi$. Local invariance of the
stable graph implies $X(0,z)=0$ throughout a small graph domain, not just at
$z=0$. The fundamental theorem of calculus gives the exact factorization
\[
 X(\xi,z)=\xi\int_0^1\partial_\xi X(t\xi,z)\,dt
         =\xi(\lambda+\alpha(\xi,z)),\qquad\alpha(0,0)=0.
\]
Let $Z(\xi,z)$ denote $\dot z$. Invariance of synchrony yields
$Z(\xi,0)=0$. Therefore with $R=Z-Bz$,
\[
 \dot z=Bz+R(\xi,z),\qquad R(\xi,0)=0,\quad D_zR(0,0)=0.
\]
On a small product neighborhood with transverse balls, the segment from
$0$ to $z$ remains in the domain and
\[
 R(\xi,z)=\int_0^1D_zR(\xi,tz)z\,dt.
\]
Continuity of $D_zR$ thus makes $\norm R\le(\gamma/2)\norm z$ uniform
over all sufficiently small $\xi$, once $\gamma>0$ is chosen below.
These are exact local equations, not an unproved linear decoupling.

\subsection{Why the nonsymmetric transverse matrix contracts (check 46)}
The key point is the \emph{orthonormal} Fourier diagonalization. Let $Q_E$
contain the nonuniform Fourier columns. Then on the complexification of $E$,
\[
 B=Q_E\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_m(p):m\ne0)Q_E^*,\qquad
 \frac{B+B^*}{2}
 =Q_E\operatorname{diag}(\Ree\lambda_m(p):m\ne0)Q_E^*.
\]
For real $z\in E$, the antisymmetric part has zero quadratic form. With
$\gamma=-\max_{m\ne0}\Ree\lambda_m(p)>0$ this proves
\[
 \langle z,Bz\rangle
 =\sum_{m\ne0}\Ree\lambda_m(p)\,|(Q_E^*z)_m|^2
 \le-\gamma\norm z^2.
\]
Although $B$ is generally nonsymmetric, normality and the invariant
orthogonal decomposition justify this Euclidean estimate. A general Hurwitz
matrix would not justify it. P10 invokes exactly the structure needed.

\subsection{Decay, sign and finite exit (checks 47--49)}
Shrink the product neighborhood so $|\alpha|<\lambda/2$ and the remainder
bound holds. Then along trajectories staying there,
\[
 \frac d{dt}\norm z^2
 =2\langle z,Bz\rangle+2\langle z,R\rangle
 \le-\gamma\norm z^2,
 \qquad \norm{z(t)}\le e^{-\gamma t/2}\norm{z(0)}.
\]
For $\xi(0)\ne0$,
\[
 \xi(t)=\xi(0)\exp\!\left(\int_0^t
       [\lambda+\alpha(\xi(s),z(s))],ds\right).
\]
This preserves its sign and gives $|\xi(t)|\ge|\xi(0)|e^{\lambda t/2}$
while in the chart. For $\xi(0)=0$, uniqueness preserves $\xi=0$.

Choose $\rho>0$ smaller than both $r_-$ and $r_+-r_-$, and small enough
for $(\pm\rho,0)$ and a compact surrounding product to lie within the
coordinate domain and the positive orthant. These two points are on the
synchronous heteroclinic branches and belong to the basins of $0$ and
$r_+\one$, respectively. Each sink has a local attracting neighborhood.
For either exit point, convergence puts its orbit in that neighborhood at
some finite time. Continuous dependence up to that time puts an open
neighborhood of the exit point in the same basin. P01 gives subsequent
global nonnegative existence. This proves relative openness of the basins,
including for the boundary sink at the origin.

Because the exit points themselves are interior to the orthant and their
basins are relatively open, select $\sigma>0$ sufficiently small that both
\emph{closed} caps
$\{(\pm\rho,z):\norm z\le\sigma\}$ lie in the corresponding basins.
The closure of the entire coordinate box can simultaneously be kept inside
the chart and positive orthant. The order is to choose the chart, then $\rho$,
then shrink $\sigma$ for both caps and compact containment.

For $\xi(0)\ne0$ in
$(-\rho,\rho)\times\{\norm z<\sigma\}$, transverse decay precludes
exit through the $z$ boundary. Sign preservation selects a unique $\xi$ cap.
If it had not exited by
\[
 T_* =\frac2\lambda\log\frac{\rho}{|\xi(0)|},
\]
the growth estimate would force $|\xi(T_*)|\ge\rho$, a contradiction.
There is no earlier hidden chart exit or finite-time loss of existence:
up to a box exit the trajectory remains in a compact subset of the smooth
open domain, and ordinary continuation applies. It therefore reaches the
signed cap in finite time and subsequently converges to its sink.
If $\xi=0$, it remains there, $z$ decays to zero, and the full state tends
to $p$. The stable disk is forward invariant in this selected box.

\subsection{Connected sides and exact relative boundary (checks 50--51)}
Let $V$ be the image of the open box and let $S$ be the image of its middle
disk $\xi=0$. The inverse chart proves $V$ is an open neighborhood of $p$;
$S$ is an embedded codimension-one open disk, relatively closed in $V$.
The two half-boxes are nonempty and connected, so their images $V_-,V_+$
are the two connected components of $V\setminus S$.
The preceding exit proof gives
\[
 V_-\subset\mathcal A(0),\qquad V_+\subset\mathcal A(r_+\one),

\]
Limits to distinct equilibria are incompatible. Since these three sets
exhaust $V$, in fact $\mathcal A(0)\cap V=V_-$ and
$\mathcal A(r_+\one)\cap V=V_+$. Every point of the middle disk can be
approached from either half by varying $\xi$, while each half is open in

\[
 \partial_V(\mathcal A(0)\cap V)
 =S=\partial_V(\mathcal A(r_+\one)\cap V).
\]
This proves dynamical two-basin separation, not merely codimension-one
topology. It does not identify the complete global boundary, the entire
continued stable manifold, or a partition of the whole orthant. Other
equilibria and periodic or other attractors elsewhere are not excluded.
P10 makes all these exclusions explicitly in lines 619--626.
