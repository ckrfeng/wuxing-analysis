## Decisive comparison

For the bare q=2 quadratic rule, choose **routine adaptation** from the four requested descriptions. The Lorenz–96 paper explicitly turns its trigonometric selection function into a quadratic in the cosine and uses equal values on either side of its vertex (accepted manuscript §4.2, pp.37–38). Changing the coefficients does not create a new optimization method. The strengthened target in this package is **structurally analogous but model-specific**: classify admissible parameter regions and connect them to first instability and a matched-law distinction. Nothing inspected justifies calling the proposed theorem genuinely stronger before those extra claims are stated and proved.

The strongest surviving candidate is item 5: a realizable, exact DM–MB contrast, supported by the selection and first-Hopf analysis. A substantive l1 sign theorem could strengthen it, but item 8 is unresolved. The four inaccessible main articles prevent an unconditional novelty claim for this package.

The most important known overlap is van Kekem–Sterk, *Travelling waves and their bifurcations in the Lorenz-96 model*, Physica D 367 (2018), 38–60, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2017.11.008). Exact locators in the supplied 55-page version: Lemma 1 and Theorem 2 p.10; Theorem 4 and Proposition 5 pp.11–12; Proposition 6 p.13; §4.2 pp.37–38; §4.3 pp.38–46. Its two-parameter extension must also be credited: Eq.(9) p.14, Eq.(10) and Lemma 8/Eq.(11) p.15, and §2.4.2 pp.16–18. These provide a particularly close precedent for switching/tie and Hopf narratives, although they do not compare our two canonical laws.
