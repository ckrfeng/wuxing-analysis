DM: ẋᵢ = axᵢ − kxᵢ² + cxᵢ₋₁/(1+xᵢ) − gxᵢ/(1+xᵢ₊q) − hxᵢxᵢ₋q.

Throughout, n≥5, 1≤q<n, all five coefficients are positive, and indices are modular. At positive DM synchrony x, put s=1+x, C=c/s, G=gx/s², H=hx and d=−kx−c(1+2x)/s². Then rₘ=d+C cosθₘ+(G−H)cos(qθₘ), with θₘ=2πm/n. “Selected” always retains the complete set of maximizers of rₘ, including ties.

PART I — Executive novelty verdict
The project remains a plausible mathematical paper, but its defensible contribution is a constrained classification of modes and interaction-law contrasts—not Fourier analysis, the golden ratio, or the generic existence of rotating waves.
The strongest current package is P05 + P11 + P08. P05 identifies every finite-ring mode that can maximize the real spectrum. P11 then cuts down that list using positivity and the equilibrium relation: some realizable dominant cells cannot contain a physical spectral boundary at all. P08 asks how these possibilities change for a specified bilinear comparator at the same nominal coefficient tuple. The resulting parity and branch restrictions are substantially more informative than a numerical demonstration that two nonlinearities behave differently.
The exact P11 iff criterion and the P08 all-size, branch-labelled comparison theorems are the leading apparent novelty candidates. No materially equivalent theorem was found in the primary sources inspected. This is a
MEDIUM-confidence, UNRESOLVED-priority-risk
judgment, because the closest five-element proceedings and thesis are not fully accessible. It is not a certificate of first publication. The positive-parameter lift of P05’s cells and switching surfaces is a meaningful supporting result. The bare nearest-cosine calculation is much less distinctive.
A close precedent is the Lorenz-96 equation ẋᵢ=xᵢ₋₁(xᵢ₊₁−xᵢ₋₂)−xᵢ+F. Its homogeneous-state real spectrum is −1+F(cosθ−cos2θ). For F>0, writing u=cosθ yields a concave quadratic with vertex 1/4. The published analysis includes finite-size first-Hopf selection, simultaneous critical pairs and explicit nonlinear criticality. This directly precludes selling a quadratic cosine dispersion or a finite-ring Hopf calculation as a new general method. The relevant locations are Lemma 1, Theorems 2/4 and Proposition 5 in the inspected preprint.
[B08: Dirk L. van Kekem, 2018]
Our additional task is different: the DM vertex moves with positive physical parameters, while its diagonal term and equilibrium are constrained by the same parameters. A freely movable quadratic vertex does not establish that all cells lift to positive coefficients, nor that a selected cell can reach r=0. Those are the mathematical distinctions that should carry the paper. P11 expresses the latter obstruction explicitly through wⱼ>0 and max(0,βⱼ)<wⱼ/[4(1−uⱼ)]. The exclusions (8,1) and (12,2) are useful because they separate two notions of realizability within the same proven selection atlas.
Even the conceptual distinction between an algebraic Hopf point and a physically feasible one has prior art. Pais, Caicedo-Núñez and Leonard explicitly discuss infeasible Hopf parameter values for circulant replicator-mutator systems after Theorem 4.5; their Lemma 4.6 also evaluates a Fourier-structured Lyapunov coefficient. This prevents a “first distinction between mathematical and admissible Hopf modes” claim. It does not supply the DM cellwise necessity-and-sufficiency theorem.
[B23: Darren Pais, 2012]
P08 has the strongest independent narrative. Its universal forward witness has x*=y*=1, so even equilibrium amplitude is matched at that point. More decisively, the reverse contrast on the specified unique-root domain exists exactly when n≡3 mod 4, while allowing the lower DM branch changes the obstruction to divisibility by four. The independent realization of every ordered pair of reachable dominant representatives with both equilibria stable further separates mode ordering from instability. These exact quantifiers and domain distinctions are what make P08 more than an example.
However, “same topology” must mean the same
unsigned directed dependency support
in this comparison. At positive states, the DM derivative with respect to xᵢ₊q is positive, while its MB analogue is negative. At q=2 these are distinct edges; for coincident offsets elsewhere, total derivatives must be combined. The comparison changes effective regulatory signs as well as receiver dependence and boundary behavior. Consequently the conclusions establish a property of these two specified laws, not an isolated biological effect of saturation with an unchanged signed interaction graph. Fixing nominal coefficients also presupposes the declared dimensionless state and time conventions.
P10 is the strongest supporting steady-state theorem. The useful calculation is Gf−Hf=rf(k+c/sf²)>0 at every positive fold, for every allowed n,q. That identity forces all transverse real parts strictly negative without extra parameter hypotheses. The subsequent simple-zero fold theorem, local sink–saddle pair, persistence of sinks and local separatrix use established machinery. Broader cyclic-feedback literature already studies bistability and basin organization; its single-input monotonicity assumptions do not automatically hold for DM.
[B25: Jules Guilberteau, 2023]
[B34: Yuri A. Kuznetsov, 2006]
The remaining current modules should mostly support those headline results. P01 establishes a sound phase space and bounds. Its exact-radius finite-entry iff statement is a small model-specific refinement; the maximum comparison method is routine, and the blueprint expressly does not claim an optimal radius. P02 is exact root algebra on synchrony. P03–P04 are differentiation and standard circulant spectral theory. P07 is a useful five-node worked example, especially for stable ties, but fifth-root golden-ratio identities are not a new biological principle. An earlier five-agent Wu-Xing paper explicitly emphasizes golden-ratio structure.
[B06: Ciann-Dong Yang, 2023]
[B17: Robert M. Gray, 2006]
P09’s full-system transcritical statement is stronger than a scalar sketch, but its ingredients are an invariant center line, a simple uniform zero and a transverse implicit-function argument. That makes it a correct routine application rather than a new bifurcation mechanism. The degenerate cubic locus should stay separately stated. P10 does not justify stability of the upper branch, index one of the lower branch, or exclusivity of two attractors throughout the entire two-root interval; the blueprint already contains an exact counterexample.
Future P12–P14 have unequal value. A nonzero total crossing derivative on the frozen g-path is necessary infrastructure. A nonzero first Lyapunov coefficient converts the spectral candidate into a classified nonlinear local bifurcation and can materially improve completeness, even if the calculation itself adds little methodological novelty. The best P13 target is an explicit sign theorem on a substantial admissible part of the Hopf surface, indexed by size or mode and connected to the P11 restrictions. The existence of some small open neighborhood of one nonzero value follows from continuity; that alone is not a strong region theorem. Fourier contraction through modes 0 and 2m already appears in generalized Lorenz-96 work.

[B21: Karen M. Page, 2018]

Stewart’s recent ring treatment gives both general phase-pattern statements and a direction criterion. The contribution of this project would be identifying which physically feasible DM modes actually produce those waves and on which parameter side, rather than introducing a new rotating-wave theorem.

PART IV — Deep audits of P05, P08, P10 and P11
P05: how much of the selection atlas survives an adversarial comparison?
KNOWN RESULT:
finite-ring spectra can be optimized on roots of unity, with a particularly close quadratic-cosine example in Lorenz-96.
OUR RESULT:
a moving positive vertex and a complete physical parameter atlas.
MINIMAL DIFFERENCE:
the variable vertex, sparse law and physical lift. The elementary optimization is a small difference; the parameter realization and its relation to criticality are structurally meaningful.
The reduction itself explains why the nearest
cosine
rule is routine. For D=H−G>0,
r(u)=d+D+C²/(8D)−2D(u−z)²,   z=C/(4D)>0.
r(uⱼ)−r(uⱼ₊₁)=C(uⱼ−uⱼ₊₁)(1−χβⱼ),   χ=4D/C.
This is one-dimensional nearest-point geometry. The midpoint equation establishes adjacency of maximal ties; three distinct cosines cannot all be equidistant from one real vertex. Once z is restricted to the positive half-line, the endpoint count J and its residues modulo four follow from the cosine grid. Calling this a new spectral optimization technique would be too strong. Calling it a complete exact classification for this constrained model is defensible.

PART XI — Recommended manuscript positioning
Recommended title:
Finite-ring mode selection and physical criticality constraints in demand-modulated cyclic networks
.
Contribution paragraph, using only current results:
We study homogeneous equilibria of a directed cyclic network with receiver-dependent input and compare them with a specified bilinear network sharing the same directed dependency support and nominal coefficients. For step-two coupling, we classify the complete finite-size dominant-mode cells and prove their realization by positive parameters. We then derive a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for a strict nonuniform cell to contain a physical spectral critical point, identifying selectable modes that cannot become critical. Matched-law comparisons yield exact stability reversals, residue-class and branch restrictions, and independent realization of dominant modes at stable equilibria. Full-system transcritical and saddle-node analysis organizes the synchronous branches, including automatic transverse stability at every positive fold. These results distinguish spectral ordering, admissible criticality and interaction-law dependence; nonlinear Hopf and rotating-wave conclusions require the additional calculations specified separately.
Six principal contributions:
The complete finite-n q=2 selection atlas with physical cell and switch realization.
The exact P11 positivity-constrained candidate criterion and explicit obstruction cells.
The all-n same-tuple forward contrast and the exact reverse parity/branch distinctions.
Independent realization of every reachable ordered pair of dominant representatives with both models stable.
The universal transverse-fold identity and a certified open region of coexistence.
The exact five-node spectral candidate, to be upgraded to a nonlinear Hopf/wave theorem only after P12–P14.
Main text:
model/comparator definition, concise framework, P05–P11 geometric relationship, P08 comparisons, branch-labelled P09/P10 organization, counterexamples and one five-node illustration. Give the closest-prior differences explicitly in the introduction.
Appendices:
extended P01 edge cases, complete negative-root tables, Jacobian collision bookkeeping, full P07 ordering/coincidence table, long constructive algebra, future tensor derivatives and normalization checks. P06 belongs here unless it reveals a genuinely additional phenomenon.
Not novelty:
a Fourier eigenbasis, invariant synchrony, generic local bifurcation theorems, the textbook l₁ formula or the equivariant Hopf conclusion. Use citations and short supporting statements.

A completed project could claim

. If P13 covers only one point, the last phrase must mean one local realization, not a global wave atlas or all admissible candidates.
