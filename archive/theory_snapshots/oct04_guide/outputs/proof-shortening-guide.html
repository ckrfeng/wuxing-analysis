Cyclic network project · Proof-to-citation guide · 4 October 2026

What to cite, what to shorten,
and what to keep proving

The main opportunity is to replace general mathematical machinery with citations, then retain the short calculation that verifies its hypotheses for DM or MB. A result can be routine without being an exact theorem in another paper. In that case, compress the algebra rather than attribute the DM statement to the wrong model.

Scope and version. This guide uses the supplied HTML prior-art audit, the archive’s earlier literature reviews, and the actual proof source files. The HTML audit itself was based on a blueprint; its judgments are checked here against those source bodies. P01–P05 and P07–P11 contain completed proofs. P06 and P12–P18 are TODO outlines in this package. Their sections below are prospective; no completed proof is claimed. P08 includes a later global-interior-stability addition. Older planning statements saying every module is TODO are stale. This is an editorial citation assessment, not a new independent mathematical audit or a certificate of novelty. The supplied project files were not modified.

How to read the recommendations. “Direct theorem application” means the general result can replace its reproof after its hypotheses are checked. “Method citation” credits a related method while retaining the DM/MB adaptation. “Internal corollary” means cite your own earlier lemma. “Retain” means the reviewed sources do not supply that model-specific claim. Exact labels below are source-file labels, so they remain usable if theorem numbers change.

Compress the general ODE machinery; retain the DM inequalities.

What still needs to remain

The exact radius, propagation of strict positivity, finite entry into K_R, and distinctions between attraction and absorption remain yours. Bușe et al. and Guilberteau are analogous applications, not replacement theorems. The audit’s textbook reference is usable for general ODE foundations, but supplies no verified theorem locator for the Dini comparison; retaining two or three lines is preferable to a loose attribution.

Suggested shortened wording

On (−1,∞)ⁿ the field is smooth, and its boundary values satisfy Fᵢ(x)|xᵢ=0=cxᵢ₋₁≥0. Thus standard ODE invariance and continuation results apply. For X=maxᵢxᵢ, direct estimation gives D⁺X≤H(X)−gX/(1+X)≤H(X), where H(X)=aX−kX²+cX/(1+X). Scalar comparison yields X(t)≤max{X(0),R}, where H(R)=0 and R>0, and hence global continuation. The additional finite-entry conclusion follows from the retained strict g-loss estimate.

Keep the shift convention, imaginary-part sign, modular collisions and both self-conjugate modes. Normality applies to homogeneous Jacobians; it cannot be silently transferred to a heterogeneous Jacobian. Zero spectral abscissa does not settle nonlinear stability.

Suggested shortened wording

By Gray (2006, Theorem 3.1), the circulant J=dI+CP+GP^(−q)−HP^q has eigenvalues λₘ=d+Ce^(−iθₘ)+Ge^(iqθₘ)−He^(−iqθₘ) in the orthonormal Fourier basis. Normality and semisimplicity follow, and unitary functional calculus gives ‖exp(tJ)‖₂=exp(t maxₘ Re λₘ). The usual linearization principles give the local nonlinear conclusions when the spectral abscissa is strictly negative or positive.

Define where every perturbed coefficient enters the model. Retain invertibility checks, the local neighborhood, admissible positivity and any quantitative radius. Uniqueness is local. A Hopf equilibrium may have invertible J even though it is not hyperbolic.

Suggested shortened wording

At the homogeneous base, det J≠0. The implicit-function theorem therefore yields a unique nearby equilibrium x*(ε) with the regularity of the specified family. Positivity follows by continuity from x*(0)>0. If J is Hurwitz, sufficiently small perturbations preserve local exponential stability by continuity of the spectrum.

What still needs to remain

Suggested shortened wording

Let γ=−max Re σ(J₀)>0. The normal-matrix perturbation bound of Embree–Trefethen implies that every perturbation E with ‖E‖₂<γ preserves Hurwitz stability. We therefore only need the stated DM estimate on E=J(ε)−J₀. Separately, at a simple transverse Hopf point, Greenbaum–Li–Overton’s derivative formula and the implicit-function theorem give g_H′(0)=−Re[p*J_εv]/Re[p*J_gv], where both derivatives include equilibrium motion.

A compact manuscript structure

Combine P01–P04 into a foundation section: one positivity/bound proposition, one synchronous-root proposition, and one Jacobian/Fourier lemma. Develop P05, P08 and P11 with their substantive constructions intact. Present P07 as a worked five-node example. Combine the general reduction tools used in P09/P10 into a short preliminary lemma; keep each model-specific nondegeneracy calculation next to its application.

Put extended weak-sign P01 cases, negative-root bookkeeping, long numerical-coincidence tables and redundant verification calculations in a supplement if they are useful for reproducibility. This is a proposed presentation change, not deletion of established claims. Do not merge all modules into a claim that Hopf or global DM dynamics are already proved.

In particular, avoid “the proof is the same as [paper]” when the paper has a different coupling law. Prefer “we apply [general theorem]; its hypotheses follow from [displayed DM identities]” or “following the method of [paper], the remaining calculation is …”.
