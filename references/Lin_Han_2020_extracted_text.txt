Analysis of Dynamic Five-Element Model
Haipeng Lin, Jing Han*
LSC, Academy of Mathematics and Systems Science, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, P. R. China 
School of Mathematical Sciences, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, P. R. China
*E-mail: hanjing@amss.ac.cn
Abstract: As the theoretical basis of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM ), five-element (Wu-Xing) model can 
generate abundant dynamic phenomena when it is considered as a dynamic system. In this paper, we extent the 
previous studied model and comprehensively consider all kinds of influence between elements, including “gain” ,  
“saturation” , “generating” , “is-generated” , “restricting” and “is-restricted”.  Based on numerical and analytical cases 
study, we find that the system will converge to a fixed point or periodic solution, or become chaotic eventually 
under different settings. The stability is studied by using the first Lyapunov’s method and the Lyapunov exponents, 
and it shows that all these cases are not stable. Additionally, by calculating the rate of change of the volume in 
phase space, we can judge this model is dissipative for “ R&isR” model, but the model with “generation” involved 
can be dissipative under specific settings.
Key Words: five-element model, generation, restriction, periodic solution, fixed point, chaotic, dissipative
1  Introduction
Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) is regarded as 
the typical wisdom of ancient Chinese. It has a holis­
tic and systematic view of human and the whole world. 
The five-element (Wu-Xing) theory [1, 2], the theoreti­
cal basis of TCM, considers these five elements (metal, 
wood, water, fire and earth) are indispensable attributes 
in the world’s construction. The relationships among 
them (see Fig.l) are mainly expressed as generation 
(also called “activation”) and restriction (also called 
“inhibition”). Each of these relationships can be di­
vided into two parts, and we take “fire” as an exam­
ple: “fire" ^  “earth""  (“fire” generates “earth”, ab­
breviated as “G ”), “w ood"" ^  “fire""  (“fire” is gen­
erated by “wood”, abbreviated as “isG”), “fire"" --*  
“m etal"" (“fire” restricts “metal” , abbreviated as “R ” ) 
and “water"  --*  “fire"" (“fire” is restricted by “water” , 
abbreviated as “isR” ). Nowadays there has been some 
diagnosis and treatment based on five-element theory, 
such as “judge the element is in either virtual or real 
state of Yin and Yang” and “fill his mother but rush 
down his son”.  If we can quantify the model from a 
mathematical perspective, it will be greatly significant 
to develop medicine research and system science.
The dynamic five-element model has abundant dy­
namic phenomena during the evolution, most of which 
can not be found in the static five-element model. Some 
mathematical approaches have been done to build and 
analyze the dynamic five-element system, which can be 
categoried as follows.
The five-element system can be built as discrete or 
continuous models. In discrete systems, states of el­
ements can be quantified into excess , normal  and 
deficiency  [3], or expressed as activity  and inactivity  
with boolen logic [4, 5]. Papers [ 6- 8]  use algebraic rea­
soning for relationships between elements. Based on the

observe the process of the evolution of the system over

Apart from four relationships mentioned, there ex­
ists another two terms: “gain” and “saturation” , which 
mean the amount of the element can increase or de­
crease with the affect of the element itself respectively. 
Only “gain” and “isG” can increase the amount of the 
element while the others play an opposite role. In the 
disposal of interactions among five elements, “isG” and 
“isR” are the basis in all approaches. Except these 
two, papers [7-11, 13-16] take “gain” and “saturation” 
into account while [ 6- 8, 11, 12, 17] consider relation­
ships “G ” and “R ”.  There are only a few approaches 
[7, 8, 11] considering all six terms simultaneously in the 
dynamic five-element models. Particularly in continu­
ous systems, the interactions between elements are set 
as linear terms ( e.g.  X  = ax)  in [16, 17], but expressed 
as cross terms (e.g. x  =  bxy) in [9-15].

In our cases study, we hnd the systems can converge 
to hxed points or periodic solutions, or become chaotic, 
but they are always unstable, and their dissipativeness 
are related to the values of parameters, including the 
initial values of hve elements as well.

2.3 Conclusion
The classic hve-element model proposed by the an­
cients is a static description of the relationships among 
hve elements. However, its applications to the human 
system and the natural laws of the world are dynamic. 
The ancients were limited by the level of mathematics at 
that time so that they could not describe the evolution­
ary dynamics of the hve-element system, but today it is 
very important for us to establish a dynamic one. In this 
paper, we create a dynamic hve-element model that in-

eludes all six terms (“gain”, “saturation”, “generating”,  
“is-generated”, “restricting” and “is-restricted”,) based 
on previous work [9], and according to the numerical 
simulation and mathematical analysis, we obtain the 
following conclusions:
• By case study, we hnd that the system may con­
verge to a hxed point or a periodic solution, or 
become chaotic under different settings of param­
eters and initial values. In particular, we prove 
that the systems converge to a fixed point in the 
cases when there are only restricting relationships 
(d > 0,h > 0,a = b  = c = g  =  0) or initial values 
of five elements are equal (x° =  x %  = ■ ■ ■  = £ 5).
• Concerning the dissipativeness of system energy, 
we prove that: ( 1 ) when the five-element system 
just has restricting relationships (i.e., d > 0,h >  
0,a = b  = c = g  =  0), it is dissipative and its en­
ergy is continuously decreasing; ( 2) when the five- 
element system just has generating relationships 
(i.e., c > 0,g > 0,a = b  = d = h  =  0), it is a 
conservative system if c = g,  or a dissipative sys­
tem if c < g , or its energy is increasing if c > g ;  
(3) when the five-element system contains “gain” 
and “saturation” (i.e., a > 0,b >  0), the system 
may be dissipative, conservative or increasing en­
ergy depending on not only the initial values of five 
elements, but also values of all parameters.
• As for the stability, the cases studied in this paper 
are all unstable.
These results preliminarily reveal the complexity of 
the dynamic five-element model and its rich dynamic 
phenomena. Further analysis is needed to obtain a more 
systematic and complete theory. In addition, the con­
struction of the model is still in theoretical speculation, 
and we should integrate it with the clinical application 
of TCM and keep imporoving it in the future.
